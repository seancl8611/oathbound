# Forge Bench

## Strand function

The Forge Bench is the weapon/prosthetic improvement station and a long-term combat-expression node.

## Lore role

The Smith's open-sided forge is built into a cliff alcove around blood-tempered steel recovered from the island. It is one of the few places capable of working corrupted material into reliable tools.

## Visual identity

- coal bed and ember glow,
- tongs and worked fragments,
- dark timber supports,
- dense practical bench,
- hammered metal scraps,
- soot and heat shimmer.

The forge reads as a warm working pocket against the Strand's cold shore.

## Interaction fantasy

The player converts hard-won materials into function: weapon improvements, prosthetic branches, sockets, and new combat options.

## Screen behavior

Use a branching forge tree with:

- clear category and branch labels,
- small, medium, and major nodes,
- resource costs,
- owned-resource totals,
- locked requirements,
- selected-node detail pane,
- distinct locked, available, previewed, purchased, and maxed states,
- room for future prosthetic sub-branches.

## Currency ownership

- **Scrolls** are the primary Forge currency.
- **Mist** may support approved general or secondary costs.
- **Boss Emblems** may gate rare major nodes.
- **Gold** is run-only and is not a normal Forge currency.

Exact prices and node requirements remain balance data.

## Presentation goal

Craft-forward and practical: the player should feel like they are engineering a hunter rather than decorating a character.

## Visual language

Dark iron, ember accents, forged silhouettes, socket shapes, and branch linework support rapid tree readability.

## Animation and environment needs

- forge ember flicker,
- heat shimmer,
- faint smoke,
- intermittent tool placement,
- active-item silhouette highlight,
- Smith work loops integrated with the station.

## Technical notes

The layout must support future branches without a complete redesign. Tooltips, prerequisites, node ranks, cost states, and controller/keyboard navigation should be planned from the start.
