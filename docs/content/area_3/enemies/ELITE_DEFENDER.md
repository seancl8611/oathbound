# Elite Defender

## Gameplay role

Area 3 defensive melee enemy and positional-pressure unit.

The Elite Defender constantly re-angles a narrow shield to deny frontal pressure and punishes overcommitment with precise short-spear thrusts.

## One-sentence fantasy

A preserved palace guard who fights like a living wall, keeping shield and spear between the player and the heart of the Court.

## Identity

Elite Defenders guard ceremonial thresholds, corridors, bridges, and inner-sanctum approaches.

Their corruption appears as impossible preservation and over-control rather than decay. Armor remains polished, posture remains exact, and every pivot resembles a perfected defensive exercise repeated beyond natural life.

Visual priorities:

- refined crimson-and-black court armor,
- fitted layered robes,
- narrow decorated shield,
- short spear,
- compact guarded silhouette,
- visibly readable shield orientation.

## Combat language

The unit should:

- maintain shield coverage while tracking the player,
- re-angle through controlled footwork,
- deny careless frontal pressure,
- use guarded advances and short thrust strings,
- punish overcommitment,
- and become vulnerable when flanked, parried, guard-broken, or committed to an attack.

The shield is the defining mechanic. It must communicate covered and uncovered angles clearly at gameplay scale.

## Revival rule

The Elite Defender does **not** use the one-time revival mechanic.

Revival already belongs to the Court Guard and Court Caster. Adding it here would dilute the Elite Defender's directional-defense identity and weaken role separation within the Area 3 roster.

## Required animation families

- idle guard,
- guarded walk or strafe,
- shield turn or re-angle,
- guarded advance,
- thrust windup,
- first thrust,
- follow-up thrust,
- block reaction,
- guard break,
- hurt,
- parried,
- stagger,
- deathblow vulnerability,
- death.

Exact frame counts, combo timing, shield coverage angles, and numerical guard behavior remain implementation and playtest work.
