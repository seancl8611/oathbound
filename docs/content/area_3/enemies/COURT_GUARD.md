# Court Guard

## Hierarchy

The Court Guard is Area 3's baseline soldier: the role-equivalent of the Corrupted Swordsman, but more refined and built around a one-time revival rule. The Elite Defender remains a separate shield-and-spear elite.

## Gameplay role

Elite baseline swordsman and revive-mechanic grunt. It pressures the player with formal sword strings, controlled timing, and a two-step kill pattern: defeat the body, then properly interrupt or finish the revival.

## One-sentence fantasy

A perfectly preserved inner-court swordsman bound by blood oath to eternal service, rising once more after death unless the player finishes the ritual properly.

## Lore context

Court Guards are inner retainers who willingly accepted Beast Blood and offered themselves to permanent service. The Blood did not destroy their discipline; it sealed it. They patrol immaculate ceremonial spaces and treat death as a temporary interruption to duty.

## Visual identity

- pristine crimson-and-black lacquer armor,
- restrained gold accents,
- clean cloth layering and polished weapon,
- formal composed posture,
- subtle red seam-light or hidden oath mark,
- pale or unnaturally still flesh where visible.

The design should be richer and more complete than earlier swordsmen without allowing ornament to obscure the attack line.

## Silhouette

Balanced, prestigious, and immediately samurai-readable: crested helmet, broad shoulder armor, layered skirt plates, poised katana, and symmetrical courtly stance. Even the revive state should remain elegant rather than becoming a shambling corpse pose.

## Weapon and attack language

- composed ready stance,
- measured slash chains,
- disciplined thrusts,
- charge or spirit-combo patterns,
- clean follow-through and poised resets,
- optional dodge or repositioning that preserves form.

The signature rule is the one-time revive. First defeat creates a vulnerable ritual state; failure to confirm the kill allows the oath to restore the Guard.

## Revive sequence

1. First lethal defeat enters a distinct fallen or kneeling preserved state.
2. Red oath-light begins returning through body or armor seams.
3. The player receives a readable interruption or finishing window.
4. Successful interruption produces final death or deathblow.
5. Failure completes a formal rise and resumes combat.

## Corruption language

Impossible upkeep, bloodless stillness, emotionless obedience, seam glow, and refusal to remain dead. The horror comes from perfect preservation and service rather than visible decay.

## Personality in motion

Exact, puppet-like, and ceremonially controlled. Steps, turns, guard transitions, and attack resets feel rehearsed. Revival is a solemn reassertion of duty rather than a desperate undead scramble.

## Combat readability

The player must understand both the refined swordsman identity and the need to confirm the kill. Initial death, revive channel, interruption window, revive completion, and final defeat must be unmistakable.

## Required animation set

- idle and poised guard,
- patrol walk and run,
- slash windup and slash strings,
- thrust windup and thrust,
- charge windup and charge,
- spirit-combo windup and sequence,
- dodge/reposition if supported,
- block,
- hurt,
- parried recoil,
- stagger/posture break,
- first death,
- revive channel/start,
- formal revive rise/complete,
- final deathblow,
- final death.

## Technical notes

The revive mechanic is the primary production requirement. Animation, VFX, audio, targeting, and encounter logic must expose the state clearly. Rich court backgrounds make silhouette clarity, sword direction, and red oath-light contrast especially important.
