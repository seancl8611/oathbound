# The Scribe

## Gameplay and narrative role

The Scribe is the Strand's archivist, recorder, and keeper of practical knowledge. She maintains the Discovery Board and catalogs enemy weaknesses, observed Technique patterns, prosthetic discoveries, Relic notes, island history, Beast Blood observations, and Akio's unprecedented Returning Blood condition.

She is an Order member assigned to the Strand for the Blood Moon expedition rather than someone who followed Akio there solely because of their relationship. She and Akio knew each other through the Order before the campaign and already share a quiet romantic attachment.

Their relationship remains understated. The Scribe is not a conventional love-interest questline and Akio has no romance choices or dialogue responses. Her emotional role is to know Akio as a person from before the island while her professional role requires her to document what the Blood is doing to him.

## Campaign starting relationship

The player does **not** meet the Scribe for the first time at game start.

Before the player's opening sequence, Akio has already reached the Strand, spoken with the expedition personnel, and departed on what everyone expects may be a one-way mission.

The first major player-facing Scribe conversation therefore occurs after Returning Blood reconstructs Akio at the Strand.

Her immediate response should be recognition, disbelief, concern, and professional alarm rather than introductory exposition:

- Akio left alive and human.
- He should not have returned from death.
- Beast Blood is now present in him.
- He has not transformed in any familiar way.

The scene should begin the mystery rather than explain it.

## Returning Blood investigation

The Scribe becomes the clearest recurring observer of Akio's condition, but she never discovers a complete scientific formula.

Across conversations and recovered records she may establish that:

- Beast Blood affects different people differently,
- some exposed people die outright,
- some become beasts or specialized forms,
- some retain more memory, identity, intelligence, or control than others,
- the curse appears to work with qualities already present in its bearer,
- resolve and discipline can matter without guaranteeing safety,
- intense drives such as greed, rage, fear, obsession, jealousy, grief, loneliness, lust, pride, or devotion may influence the resulting form,
- Akio's Blood is rebuilding him rather than simply consuming him,
- and it becomes increasingly responsive to his intention and instinct.

Useful thematic observations include:

> "The Blood does not make beasts from nothing. It works with what it finds."

and, after enough repeated returns:

> "I don't think the Blood is choosing the Strand, Akio. I think you are."

These lines are working intent, not mandatory final script.

The Scribe may be the character who gives **Returning Blood** its practical in-world name once repeated reconstruction is undeniable.

## Why the Strand observation matters

The Scribe should eventually infer that the Strand is not a magical resurrection anchor.

Akio increasingly directs Returning Blood while conscious. When he dies, that conscious control disappears and the Blood falls back on survival instinct. The Strand is the safest place Akio knows during the expedition, so he subconsciously reconstructs at the same established location.

The Scribe can reach this conclusion through pattern recognition rather than mystical certainty.

## Relationship to Akio

Akio and the Scribe met while serving within the Order. Their bond developed before either was stationed at the Strand, giving Akio an established human connection and implied life outside the island mission.

The relationship is romantic but restrained: familiar concern, trust, shared history, and quiet intimacy rather than a large romance plot.

After Returning Blood reconstructs him, relief is mixed with fear and professional uncertainty. She does not treat resurrection as a gift simply because it keeps him alive.

Across the campaign she can question what repeated crossings and Blood use are costing him while still helping interpret discoveries and supporting the mission. She represents a life and relationship Akio could value outside the island; she does not command him to continue or stop.

## One-sentence fantasy

A gentle but guarded young Order scribe who preserves the island's truths in careful records while trying to understand the impossible curse now rebuilding someone she already cares about.

## Visual identity

A young woman in layered, weathered recorder's robes suited to the Strand's cold wind and long working nights. Her design should feel scholarly, practical, and quietly human rather than noble or ceremonial:

- soft dark fabrics and faded wraps,
- layered outer robe or cloak,
- belts, pouches, tags, cords, scroll cases, brushes, and writing tools,
- journal, ledger, or record bundles.

She should feel warm and approachable without appearing fully open; restraint and guarded knowledge remain part of the presentation.

## Silhouette

Slim, softly vertical, and immediately scholarly:

- long draped outer layer,
- book or ledger as a strong prop,
- hanging tags and writing tools,
- light waist-hung record materials,
- clear head-and-shoulder separation.

She should read as a record-keeper, not a merchant, fighter, or shrine maiden.

## Personality in motion

Calm, kind, attentive, and precise. She handles paper and tools carefully, thinks before speaking, and reveals guardedness through small hesitations, measured eye contact, and controlled stillness rather than dramatic secrecy.

## Idle and interaction needs

Required states:

- standing or seated record idle,
- reading and writing variants,
- page turn,
- paper sorting and tying,
- board-note adjustment,
- thoughtful pause,
- interaction acknowledgment,
- restrained talk loop,
- gesture toward Discovery Board,
- optional Raven-care or message-tie ambient beat.

Routine discoveries should feel practical. Deeper truths may use a lowered gaze, pause, or reduced motion to show trust being measured.

## Technical notes

- Prioritize clean robe layering and readable book, brush, scroll, and tag props.
- The Discovery Board and study materials are part of her character presentation.
- Use shape grouping rather than tiny detail.
- Keep her grounded and practical instead of mystical.
- Thematic emphasis is preserved knowledge, gentle competence, guarded trust, and incomplete understanding.
