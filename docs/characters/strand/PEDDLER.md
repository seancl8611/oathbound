# The Peddler

## Gameplay and narrative role

The Peddler is the Strand's merchant of necessity and discomfort. He sells salvaged goods, relics, charms, armor fragments, prosthetic materials, and other useful objects recovered from the island's dead, ruins, and wreckage. He represents the practical economy that grows around catastrophe: someone has learned to sort, clean, price, and sell what others still regard as tragedy.

## One-sentence fantasy

A calm and courteous scavenger-merchant who turns the island's dead history into useful inventory before anyone else is ready to admit it is salvage.

## Visual identity

A grounded human merchant surrounded by curated clutter:

- relic bundles,
- beads and charms,
- wrapped tools,
- armor scraps,
- scrolls,
- trinkets,
- portable stock and small candlelight.

His layered clothing should feel traveled, patched, weather-worn, and practical rather than noble or martial. Pouches, cords, tags, and draped wraps should make him feel like a mobile shrine-market without turning him into a mystic.

## Silhouette

Low, compact, and horizontal. A seated human figure sits inside a broad spread of goods and cloth layers. The defining read is a trader nested within curated salvage. Even when partially rising, he should remain wrapped and gear-laden rather than elegant or combat-ready.

## Personality in motion

His hands are precise and practiced. He sorts, unwraps, weighs, inspects, and presents objects without reverence or disgust. He is polite, attentive, mildly amused, and unsettlingly comfortable with provenance he does not fully explain.

## Idle and interaction needs

Required presentation states:

- seated merchant idle,
- relic inspection or bundle retying,
- stock adjustment,
- charm handling,
- small head movements toward the player or camp,
- interaction acknowledgment,
- practical talk loop,
- item-indicating gesture,
- slight forward lean for an offer,
- shop-focused posture.

Optional ambient support includes candle flicker, cloth sway, trinket movement, or a brief moment of studying an item before returning it to stock.

## Technical notes

- Treat the surrounding stock as part of the character presentation, not random background clutter.
- Use modular prop layers where they improve reuse and readability.
- Prioritize large shape groupings over tiny detail.
- Keep him trustworthy enough to transact with, while preserving the implication that he is unusually comfortable doing business at the edge of mass death.
